# Source: gig repertoire — three songs, chords + lyrics

> In a live project this file is `.resurface/source.md`. Captured once; both
> surfaces (letter sheets, index cards) decide against it.

## Intent

This repertoire exists so the player can **perform three songs without
stopping**. Eyes drop to the page only at chord changes and verse entrances;
both hands are on the guitar the entire time. Anything that forces a page
turn, a card flip, or a squint mid-phrase defeats the artifact.

## Audience & use context

One guitarist (the author). Two contexts, one per surface:

- **Music stand at home / open mic** — sheet sits 60–90 cm from the eyes,
  decent light, stand holds letter paper well. Hands never free.
- **Song circle / jam** — no stand; cards balance on a knee, a case, or grass.
  40–60 cm viewing, variable light, wind is real. Cards must be stiff, small,
  and self-ordering (title visible at a glance).

## Content model

| Tier | Content | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **T1** | Chord symbols, positioned over the syllable where the change lands | Wrong placement = wrong change = stopped song |
| **T1** | Lyric lines, broken at sung phrase boundaries | A break mid-phrase makes the eye stumble exactly when it's needed most |
| **T1** | Key, tempo, time signature, capo position | First three seconds of every song |
| **T2** | Repeated choruses | Known by heart after one pass; condensable to a cue line that preserves the form map |
| **T2** | Performance notes ("ritard the last line", "off the stop") | Helpful, condensable to a few words at the cue |
| **T3** | Verse numbering, songwriting notes, alternate voicings | Drop everywhere |

The three songs:

1. **The Long Meander** — river song. G major, ♩=76, 4/4. Three verses, one
   chorus. Lines run 28–46 characters. 16 sung lines + chorus repeats.
2. **Last Bus Out of Calloway** — leaving-town song. C major **played capo 2**
   (sounds in D), ♩=132, 4/4. Three verses, chorus, bridge with a stop. The
   long one: 22 sung lines, lines up to 52 characters.
3. **The Orchard Waltz** — 3/4, D major, ♩=96. Three verses, one chorus.
   16 sung lines, but waltz phrasing runs long: lines up to 56 characters.

## Design language

None inherited — the songs lived in a plain-text file. Established for the
set: a warm, readable text serif for lyrics (**Source Serif 4** — tall
x-height, survives distance), **Archivo** bold for chords and structural
labels (chords must pop off the lyric line in peripheral vision), **Fraunces**
for titles (the one place character is free). Black on white only; these get
photocopied.

## Voice

Plainspoken American folk. Concrete nouns, no ornament. Cue lines and
performance notes should sound like a musician's pencil marks ("chorus ×2,
end on G"), not like software ("repeat refrain 2 times").
