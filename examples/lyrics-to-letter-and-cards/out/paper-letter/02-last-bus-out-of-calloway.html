Last Bus Out of Calloway

Key C · Capo 2♩ = 1324/4drive the downstrokes
The diner light goes amber
at a quarter after nine,
Earl stacks up the chairs
and pockets up his keys.
There's a duffel in the gravel
and a ticket in my hand,
and a wind that smells like
anywhere but here.
Mama said this town will hold you
like a too-small coat,
warm enough to make you
stay too long.
I left a note by the coffee pot
with the truth and nothing else:
some birds are only
borrowing the barn.
Chorus
Last bus out of Calloway,
headlights through the rain.
Everything I'm keeping
fits the rack above my seat.
The driver takes my dollar, says
“Son, I have seen that look —
half of this whole county
wears it well.”
We roll past the old water tower,
my name in faded paint,
and I don't turn around
to watch it disappear.
— chorus —
Bridge
If I make it big or make it broke,
either way I'll send
a postcard home.
The hardest part of leaving Calloway
is how easy it turns out to be.  
— chorus · last line ×2, off the stop —
