02

Six steps, each on the record

.resurface/ — durable artifacts
1

Capture

Why does this exist? Who uses it, where, with what in their hands? Content gets survival tiers: T1 must live, T2 can condense, T3 can drop.

.resurface/source.md
2

Choose

You name a surface. The agent loads its profile: real dimensions, color depth, refresh, ink economy, failure modes — hardware truth, not vibes.

surfaces/*.md
3

Decide

A written decision record: layout in real units, rewrites shown, idioms chosen. Low-confidence calls come back as multiple choice — once, then recorded forever.

.resurface/decisions/<surface>.md
4

Render

A surface-native artifact: print-CSS HTML, 1-bit raster, ESC/POS bytes, fixed-pixel PNG, slides with notes.

.resurface/out/<surface>/
5

Proof

Rasterized at the device’s true pixels and bit depth — 384px wide and 1-bit if that’s what the printer sees — then inspected. Nothing ships on “should work.”

proof.png · inspected
6

Deliver

CUPS, ESC/POS, TRMNL, OpenEPaperLink, PDF, kiosk. Add cron: your day is a sticker on the printer before you’re awake.

connectors/*.md

03

House rules

editorial judgment, codified
RULE 01

Physical truth.

Real units, from the profile, never from memory. A 58mm receipt is 384 dots. An index card is 3×5 inches. A “12pt glyph” across the kitchen is not 12pt.

RULE 02

Use-context beats screen size.

The guitarist can’t turn a page mid-song. The cook’s hands are wet. Context decides; geometry only constrains.

RULE 03

Never truncate. Re-edit.

An ellipsis is a failure. Rewriting eight requirements as five tighter ones, in the author’s voice, is the job.

RULE 04

Spend the whole budget.

Whitespace on a printed lyric sheet is wasted fidelity — scale up until the content fits the surface, not just on it.

RULE 05

Translate idioms.

Checkboxes on retina; strikethroughs on thermal; outline boxes where gray doesn’t exist; QR codes where links can’t live.

RULE 06

Proof like it’s hardware.

Rasterize at true pixels and bit depth. Look at it. Fix it. Print one before printing twenty.

House rules04 / 08

Never truncate. Re-edit.

NOTES The framework's hardest rule. Dumb truncation is what deterministic pipelines do; an editor rewrites. Eight requirements become five better ones — and the rewrite is shown in the decision record, where the author can veto it.
